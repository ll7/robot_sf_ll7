# Qualified summary of the bounded bicycle diagnostic

This is a development-seed diagnostic on the 0.0.8 release code, not release evidence or an admitted thesis claim. The comparison uses one matched bicycle geometry, three planners and six fixed cells. Its reported success and collision differences and planner ordering are **equal-cell pooled** results; they are not claims about every cell or untested planners, plants, seeds or scenarios.

Pooled ORCA and goal success and collision differences are within about 3 percentage points, and the pooled ordering is ORCA > social force > goal on both plants. In the ordinary doorway, however, goal success changes from 18/30 DD to 16/30 BI (−6.7 percentage points), and collision increases by 6.7 points. Social force falls from 26/30 to 10/30 (−53.3 percentage points, or 61.5% fewer successes relative to DD). Social force versus goal therefore has a **descriptive point-estimate reversal in this cell**: 26/30 versus 18/30 on DD and 10/30 versus 16/30 on BI. The post-hoc seed-block interval for the BI difference includes zero, so this is not a resolved reversal under the preregistered rule.

In the empty-world probes, ORCA and social force stall with the bicycle at the tested 90°, 135° and 180° bearings, and goal stalls at the tested 180° bearing. These probes do not establish stalls at every large heading change or a general condition for transfer. The yaw-only requests support a tested controller/interface incompatibility, but do not distinguish planner logic from adapter behavior or the kinematic change from the yaw-cap asymmetry. A bicycle success witnesses reachability only for its particular tested initial state.

The corrected analyzer applies the complete preregistered O2 rule. Reanalysis of the archived rows changes no O1/O2/O3/O4/O5 classification: no equal-cell pooled planner pair has a resolved reversal. Numeric counts, intervals, and classifications are in [summary.json](summary.json) and [paired_effects.csv](paired_effects.csv); the frozen contract and scope are described in the [packet README](../README.md).
